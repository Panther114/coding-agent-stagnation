## 2. Required by the competition, not yet done (you must do these)

1. **Cover page** with names, school province/country, advisor name(s), report title. The
   template is linked from the rules page ("下载研究报告模板").
2. **Acknowledgement page (1–2 pages, 500–1500 words)** covering: research background; the
   relationship between advisor and students; what the advisor did and whether it was paid; what
   help came from others; and *a detailed division of labour* across topic selection, data
   acquisition, analysis/computation, experimental design and writing. The AI-use rules also
   require a **detailed, truthful disclosure of AI use**: tool names and versions, which stages,
   for what purpose, when, and how often.
3. **AI chat records** must be uploaded as supporting material if AI was used.
4. **Academic integrity declaration**, signed by students and advisor and stamped by the school
   (and by the outside institution if the advisor is external).
5. **Advisor information form**, signed and stamped (1–2 advisors).
6. **Plagiarism report** for the final PDF (CNKI / PaperPass etc.); above 10% similarity means
   disqualification. Upload it before the deadline.
7. **Optional but explicitly encouraged for Computer Science**: attach the executable code, a
   short screen-recording of the pipeline running, and the raw result tables — the CS criteria
   mention "建议提交视频、可执行程序包、源代码等资料进行真实性证明".

## 3. Scientific decisions that are yours, not the agent's

The evaluation criteria reward **originality of the question and of the method**, and the rules
require that the core argument and experimental validation be the students' own work. Three
things in particular should be your call, because they change the paper's meaning:

* **Whether to keep the negative result as the headline.** The strongest result here is that
  task-grounded evidence does not beat a semantic-redundancy baseline, and that most of the
  apparent accuracy is between-run rather than within-run. Reporting it is more defensible than
  rescuing the original hypothesis, but the framing is yours to choose.
* **The stagnation definition (Section 3).** It is operational and falsifiable, but a different
  definition would produce different numbers. If you disagree with a channel, change the
  codebook and re-run — that is a legitimate and interesting study.
* **Which operating point to recommend.** The paper deliberately refuses to name one. If you
  want to recommend a threshold, the curves in `window_alarm_frontier.json` give you the
  trade-off and the false-stop cost.

## 4. Verify these five things yourself before submitting

1. Run `python scripts/run_experiments.py` yourself and confirm the AUC numbers match
   `results/final/tb2_final/window_metrics.json`.
2. Open 20 random annotation cards in `research/data/annotations/tb2/cards_dense/` and check
   whether you agree with the stored label. Report your agreement rate in the paper: it
   converts the study's biggest weakness into a result.
3. Check that every citation in `paper/refs.bib` resolves to a real paper by opening the URL.
   Two were reachable only as landing pages (the AIware paper) and are cited accordingly.
4. Re-read the AI disclosure against `research/AI_ASSISTANCE_LOG.md` and make sure it matches
   what actually happened, in your own words.
5. Check the report is under the similarity threshold *before* uploading the final version.

## 5. If you have one more day

Add the intervention experiment: run a handful of Terminal-Bench tasks, and when the monitor
alarms, stop and restart with the current diff offered as an overlay. That single experiment
would answer the question the current paper can only raise — whether an alarm at a given
false-stop rate actually improves task completion rather than merely removing steps.
