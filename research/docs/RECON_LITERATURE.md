# Literature Reconnaissance — "Coding agents fail by being WRONG rather than LOST"

**Compiled:** 2026-09-13
**Scope:** Prior art around (a) the claim that agent failure is a *fix* failure rather than a *localisation/search* failure, (b) benchmarks/detectors that classify agent failure modes, (c) published state of the art for detecting that a coding agent is failing, and (d) wasted/dead-end edit measurement.
**Method:** `web_search`, Exa (`web_search_exa`, `web_search_advanced_exa`, `web_fetch_exa`), arXiv abstract pages, arXiv HTML full text, Semantic Scholar, local raw corpus in `research/docs/_raw/`.
**Verification policy:** Every arXiv ID below was resolved to a title by fetching `arxiv.org/abs/<id>` or its HTML, or by reading the cached raw file. Anything not confirmed is explicitly marked **UNVERIFIED**. No citation, author, or number below is reconstructed from memory.

## 2. VERDICT ON NOVELTY OF THE ON-TARGET REVERSAL

### 2.1 The claim, restated precisely

> On 263 SWE-bench-style tasks that contain **both** a solved and a failed run, failed runs place a **higher fraction of their edits** in the file that ends up in the final patch (**0.620**) than solved runs do (**0.576**); within-instance paired comparison, **p = 3.3e-4**.

### 2.2 Verdict

**The on-target reversal — failed runs localising BETTER than solved runs, on the same task — is NOT reported anywhere I could find. It appears to be NOVEL.** I state this unambiguously.

I searched, across `web_search` and three Exa tools, for: failed runs localising better / higher on-target fraction in failed runs / "wrong fix" / "correct file, wrong change" / within-task paired localisation comparison / reversed localisation–outcome correlation / "lost vs wrong". No paper reports a directional, statistically-tested reversal of the localisation–outcome relationship under a within-instance or within-task control.

**However — four qualifications, and they are load-bearing. Do not present the novelty claim without them.**

**Q1 — The *interpretation* of the reversal is already published, twice, and must be cited.** The step "failed runs reached the right code, therefore the failure is in the fix, not the search" is independently established:
- [2603.24631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24631): *"The dominant failure of capable models is **not localization**: 60–69% of failures … reach and edit the correct functions yet still produce incorrect patches."* This is our conclusion, at larger scale (16,758 trajectories), from a different measurement (bucket assignment by recall threshold, not an edit-fraction ratio).
- [2511.00197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00197): Finding 7 states the majority of failing trajectories locate the correct file and that *"failures often stem from fine-grained reasoning rather than coarse repository navigation."*

Our contribution must therefore be scoped to **the reversal as a quantitative, within-instance, statistically-tested phenomenon** — not to the qualitative "wrong, not lost" thesis, which is prior art.

**Q2 — The *methodological* move (within-task paired comparison reversing a cross-task finding) is already published.** [2604.02547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02547) runs an essentially identical two-approach design on 19 agents × 500 SWE-bench Verified tasks and reports that the cross-task "failures are longer" effect **reverses** within-task (resolved 44.0 vs failed 39.6 steps; 263/416 tasks; p = 1.9e-9). They do this for **trajectory length**, not for on-target edit fraction. So: the design is prior art; the *application of that design to localisation* is not. Note also that our study has 263 tasks and theirs has 263 of 416 tasks with a positive delta — a coincidence worth double-checking in our own pipeline, since a reviewer may read it as a copy.

**Q3 — Two papers report the OPPOSITE direction on adjacent metrics, and a reviewer will find them.**
- [2608.08570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08570) FailForge Table 7 reports "localization precision … on solved (pass) vs. unsolved (fail) instances", and **solved is higher in all five rows** (Base 0.46 vs 0.38). This is the closest published analogue to our metric, under a related-but-different definition (fraction of *edited source files* that are gold-target files, evaluated on the submitted patch) — and it points the other way.
- [2511.00197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00197) reports file-level gold-match of **success >90% vs failure 59.3–81.4%**, also the other way.

Two defensible reconciliations must appear in the paper, because the difference between our metric and theirs is real and non-cosmetic:
1. **Unit of analysis.** Ours is the fraction of *edit operations across the trajectory* landing in the eventual final-patch file. Both of the above measure a *property of the submitted/final patch* (or of its file set). A run can make 50 edits in the right file, submit a patch in the right file, and still fail — and both metrics would look different. State the unit explicitly in every figure caption.
2. **Outcome gate.** Our population is restricted to instances with **both** a solved and a failed run, and the comparison is within-instance. Theirs are pooled across instances with heterogeneous difficulty. The whole point of [2604.02547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02547) is that pooling across tasks of varying difficulty flips the sign of such effects. Expect the objection "you would get the naive direction if you did not control for task", and pre-empt it by reporting the uncontrolled cross-instance version alongside, as [2604.02547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02547) does.

**Q4 — TraceProbe reports failed-vs-resolved divergence in file scope, against us.** [2607.06184](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06184v1) aligns failed runs to resolved references on the same task and finds **File Selection divergence of 38.5–95.0%**, usually the largest of its three layers. That is a same-task, paired, file-level signal pointing the *opposite* way. Their own framing softens this ("file choice is too coarse to separate success from failure"; "some runs work in the wrong file scope, some reach the right file but miss the relevant function, and some reach the right function but still make the wrong edit"), but their number is a genuine headwind. Their metric is divergence-from-a-reference rather than absolute on-target fraction, so the two are not strictly comparable — again, say so explicitly rather than ignoring it.

### 2.3 Safe novelty statement

> Prior work establishes that capable coding agents usually reach the correct code and still fail [2603.24631, 2511.00197], and it has used within-task paired designs to overturn cross-task behavioural findings [2604.02547]. **We are not aware of any prior work that measures the fraction of an agent's edits landing in the file that ends up in its final patch, splits it by outcome, and reports a within-instance reversal in which failed runs are MORE on-target than solved runs.** Papers measuring the analogous quantity at the level of the submitted patch report the opposite direction [2608.08570, 2511.00197], which makes the reversal a claim about *trajectory-level* behaviour that patch-level metrics cannot see.

### 3.4 Statistical bar

- Our p = 3.3e-4 on 263 within-instance pairs must be presented against [2604.02547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02547)'s **p = 1.9e-9** (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank, 263/416 tasks positive) — same design, same test family, much stronger. We should also report **Cliff's δ** and the **fraction of tasks with a positive delta**, as they do, not just a p-value.
- **Attend to the noise floor.** [2607.09691](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09691) reports that temperature-0 API inference flips **~9%** of per-instance outcomes between byte-identical runs on SWE-bench Verified. Any within-instance outcome split inherits this. We should state how within-instance solved/failed labels were obtained and how many runs per instance, or a reviewer will use this to discount the effect.

## 4. The five most important papers

1. **Coherence Collapse: Diagnosing Why Code Agents Fail After Reaching the Right Code** — [arXiv:2603.24631](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24631) — *the* prior-art paper for our thesis: 60–69% of capable-model failures reach and edit the correct function and still fail, with "Confused Thrashing" (29.1%) and "Near-Correct Corrupted" (10.6%) as the closest published analogues to our dead-end edits. **We must position against this paper or be scooped by it.**

2. **Understanding Code Agent Behaviour: An Empirical Study of Success and Failure Trajectories** — [arXiv:2511.00197](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00197) (ICSE 2026) — *"Failures often stem from fine-grained reasoning rather than coarse repository navigation"*: the closest qualitative statement of "wrong, not lost", with file-level gold-match numbers (success >90%, failure 59.3–81.4%) that run counter to our reversal and must be reconciled.

3. **Beyond Resolution Rates: Behavioral Drivers of Coding Agent Success and Failure** — [arXiv:2604.02547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02547) — the methodological template and the direct precedent for reporting a within-task paired **reversal** (44.0 vs 39.6 steps; 263/416 tasks; p = 1.9e-9); also the source of the "control for task difficulty or your sign flips" objection we must pre-empt.

4. **TRIM: Reducing AI-Generated CodeSlop via Agent Trajectory Minimization** — [arXiv:2607.18161](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18161) — defines CodeSlop and reports **20.0%** unnecessary patch content on SWE-bench (17.9–32.9% across scaffolds), the single number our **19.1%** dead-end edit rate must be differentiated from.

5. **What Resolve Rate Hides: Trajectory Structure Diagnostics for Coding Agents (TraceProbe)** — [arXiv:2607.06184](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06184v1) — the closest existing trajectory-diagnostic framework, with per-setting **Justified %** (32.0–41.7%) as the nearest published "on-target" quantity, and same-task file-selection divergence (38.5–95.0%) as the main headwind against our reversal.

**Runner-ups worth a sentence each in related work:**

- **Confident and Wrong: Silent Semantic Failures in Coding Agents** — [arXiv:2603.25764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25764) — reframes our thesis as a **monitoring** problem: GPT-5 submits on 100% of runs and resolves 44%, and completion-based *and* consistency-based monitors both look healthy precisely on the runs that should not be trusted. If our paper claims anything about detecting failure, this is the paper to position against.
- **FailForge** — [arXiv:2608.08570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08570) — because its Table 7 reports pass-vs-fail localization precision in the **opposite direction** to us, and a reviewer who finds it will ask why. Better to raise it ourselves.
- **Who&When** — [arXiv:2505.00212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00212) — the shared benchmark for failure attribution; cite it as the field's evaluation substrate (53.5% agent-level / 14.2% step-level ceiling) if we claim any attribution capability.
