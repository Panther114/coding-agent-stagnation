\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Coding agents fail, and a large literature tries to detect \emph{when}. This study asks a prior
question: \textbf{are we measuring the right thing?} From 236{,}137 mechanically labelled edit
operations across 25{,}681 agent runs on 1{,}213 software-engineering instances we report four
results, one of which corrects our own earlier headline.

\textbf{(1) A measurement blind spot.} A commonly computable statistic --- the share of a run's
edits aimed at files in \emph{that run's own final patch} --- inverts the sign of the
solved-versus-failed comparison. It reports failed runs localising \emph{better} ($0.673$ vs
$0.585$); against an \textbf{independent gold patch} the direction reverses ($0.477$ vs $0.407$).
The metric is a closed loop, so a run that fixates on the wrong file and never wavers scores $1.0$.
Two published papers report the direction our correction recovers, and we tested three mechanisms
for the inversion --- \emph{all three failed}, so the defect is demonstrated but not explained.

\textbf{(2) Localisation is bounded.} The robust measure is binary --- did the run ever edit a
gold-patch file --- and it is positive in every patch-width stratum: $98.2\%$ of successful runs and
$69.1\%$ of failed runs reach the correct file. Localisation can therefore address at most the
\textbf{$30.9\%$} of failures that never reach it.

\textbf{(3) The causal test.} Holding a defect fixed and varying \emph{only} how easy it is to find:
handing over the exact file and function lifts success from $23.5\%$ to $43.8\%$, a failure-rate drop
of $0.203$ that is \emph{below} the $0.309$ ceiling the observational result predicted in advance.
Decisively, \textbf{even when the agent reached the correct file --- $82\%$ of runs did --- it
succeeded only $28.6\%$ of the time.} A re-diagnosis prompt did not help.

\textbf{(4) A working router.} From the first $10$--$60\%$ of a run we predict whether a struggling
agent needs \emph{search} or \emph{verification}, reaching $0.676$--$0.751$ at separating lost from
wrong-fix runs, where every published-style heuristic (loop, burst, redundancy, output-shape) sits
\textbf{at or below chance} ($0.407$--$0.539$). Its false-alarm rate is genuinely controlled
($\alpha=0.05 \rightarrow 0.046$ achieved), unlike a circular calibration claim we withdrew; as a
routing policy it is worth $0.764$--$0.800$ against $0.732$ for always-verify and $0.518$ for
always-search.

We report a ledger of \textbf{eight retracted claims, three of them our own headlines}, and we state
plainly that the qualitative interpretation --- that capable agents fail \emph{after} reaching the
right code --- is prior art, independently reproduced here rather than discovered.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\hangindent=2em\textbf{Keywords:} coding agents, failure analysis, measurement validity,
runtime monitoring, reproducibility, honest reporting.

\section{Introduction}

Coding agents resolve a substantial share of real software issues and fail on the rest. Much work
treats failure as a \emph{monitoring} problem: watch the trajectory, notice when the agent is stuck,
intervene. That framing has a hidden premise --- that the agent fails because it is \textbf{lost},
unable to find the right place to work.

This study began with a startling result supporting the opposite view. On tasks containing both a
solved and a failed run, failed runs appeared to aim a \emph{higher} share of their edits at the file
in their final patch ($0.620$) than solved runs did ($0.576$), $p = 3.3\times10^{-4}$. If failures
localise at least as well as successes, localisation is not the bottleneck and a great deal of
monitoring research is aimed at the wrong target.

That result is wrong, and \S\ref{sec:blind} shows why. But the way it is wrong, and what stands in
its place, are more useful than the original claim.

We had two advantages. First, the framework we study prints a structural footer into its
observations --- \code{[File: /abs/path/to/file.py (N lines total)]} --- in $95.8\%$ of edit steps.
That lets us label every edit \emph{mechanically}, with no human judgement and no model in the loop:
whether the file changed, by how many lines, and whether those lines ever reach the agent's final
patch. The study moves from \emph{``we asked a model what stagnation looks like''} to \emph{``we
counted which written lines shipped.''}

Second, we obtained \textbf{independent ground truth}: gold patches for $927$ of $1{,}213$
instances, from the upstream SWE-rebench and SWE-bench corpora. That target does not depend on
anything an agent produced --- and it is what exposed the blind spot.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) a measurement blind spot, demonstrated with both metrics on identical
edits and a control showing they agree where they must (\S\ref{sec:blind}); (ii) a bounded
localisation result, robust across every stratum, replacing a retracted claim
(\S\ref{sec:where}); (iii) a causal test with localisation solved by fiat (\S\ref{sec:causal});
(iv) a router that beats every baseline on a target where the field's detectors have no signal, with
genuine false-alarm control (\S\ref{sec:router}); (v) a retraction ledger (\S\ref{sec:retract}).

\section{Related work, and what is not ours}
\label{sec:related}

We verified every citation below by fetching it directly; automated reconnaissance summaries were
not trusted for load-bearing claims.

\paragraph{The interpretation is not ours.} \emph{Coherence Collapse}
\cite{coherencecollapse2026} reports across 16{,}758 trajectories that \emph{``the dominant failure
of capable models is not localization: 60--69\% of failures on SWE-Agent and OpenHands reach and
edit the correct functions yet still produce incorrect patches.''} Our independently measured
\textbf{$67.1\%$ sits inside that range}. A second study \cite{majgaonkar2025behaviour} finds the
majority of failing trajectories locate the correct files. We claim \textbf{no novelty} for the
qualitative finding, and say so rather than re-deriving it.

\paragraph{Two papers report the opposite direction on adjacent metrics.} FailForge
\cite{failforge2026} reports localisation precision higher for passing than failing runs, and
TraceProbe \cite{traceprobe2026} finds same-task file-selection divergence in failed runs. These
agree with \textbf{our corrected} result and disagree with the metric we expose in
\S\ref{sec:blind}. Reconciling an apparent contradiction in the literature is part of the
contribution.

\paragraph{The monitoring framing is not ours either.} \emph{Confident and Wrong}
\cite{confidentwrong2026} shows GPT-5 submitting a patch on $100\%$ of runs while resolving $44\%$,
and observes that \emph{``completion-based and consistency-based monitoring both look healthy
exactly when the agent should not be trusted.''} Our finding that the \emph{most} fixated runs are
precisely those that never reached the right file (\S\ref{sec:blind}) independently corroborates
that warning.

\paragraph{Waste has been measured before.} TRIM \cite{trim2026} reports $20.0\%$ ``CodeSlop'' on
SWE-bench, close to our $19.1\%$ dead-end rate. The objects differ and we state the difference: TRIM
measures unnecessary \emph{lines in a passing patch}, established by counterfactual re-execution; we
measure edit \emph{operations} that never reach the patch, with no execution. Ours is cheaper and
coarser; theirs is causally stronger.

\paragraph{The detection bar is soft.} Published detectors sit at AUC $0.6$--$0.7$
\cite{agentstop2025}; the field's shared attribution benchmark reaches $53.5\%$ agent-level but only
$14.2\%$ step-level \cite{whowhen2025}; a failure-as-a-process monitor achieves $82\%$ precision at
$2$--$3\%$ false alarms but only $18.2\%$ recall \cite{failureprocess2026}; the step-level
redundancy ceiling is $24.88\%$ \cite{redundancybench2026}.

\paragraph{What is ours} is the measurement result: no located paper reports failed runs localising
\emph{better} within-instance, and a broken self-referential metric is exactly what produces that
sign.

\section{The measurement blind spot}
\label{sec:blind}

\subsection{The statistic}
The conventional localisation statistic is
\[
\text{on-target} \;=\; \frac{\text{edits aimed at files the run's final patch touches}}{\text{edits}},
\]
Both the numerator's file set and the denominator come from \textbf{the same run}. It asks
\emph{``did the run converge on what it produced?''}, not \emph{``did it aim at the right place?''}
A run that fixates on the wrong file and never wavers scores $1.0$.

\subsection{Replacing the target with ground truth}
We obtain gold patches for $927$ of $1{,}213$ instances ($843$ SWE-rebench, $84$ SWE-bench) and
recompute on \textbf{identical edit steps}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
measure & solved & failed & reading \\
\midrule
on-target, own patch (\emph{the conventional statistic}) & $0.594$ & $\mathbf{0.670}$ & failed ``better'': the startling result reproduces \\
on-target, \textbf{gold} patch (basename)               & $\mathbf{0.477}$ & $0.407$ & direction reverses \\
on-target, \textbf{gold} patch (path match)             & $\mathbf{0.510}$ & $0.430$ & robust to the match rule \\
\textbf{ever reached a gold file}                       & $\mathbf{0.982}$ & $0.670$ & large and unambiguous \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The same edit steps under two target definitions, \emph{pooled over runs}
(\code{wrongness.json}). The conventional statistic and the independent one disagree in
\emph{sign}. Within-instance paired means for the gold target are $0.496$ solved vs $0.448$ failed
($p=0.007$, $228$ instances).}
\end{table}

\subsection{A control that both metrics are sane}
Restrict to runs whose own patch is a \emph{single} file that \emph{is} a gold file. There the two
target sets must coincide, and they do: $0.615$ vs $0.638$, mean absolute gap $0.033$
($n = 8{,}801$). Neither definition is broken; they measure different things.

\subsection{The robust measure, and why the shares are not}
Both \emph{shares} are fragile to patch composition; the \emph{binary} measure is not.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccr}
\toprule
own patch width & $n$ solved & $n$ failed & ever reached gold (solved) & (failed) & $\Delta$ \\
\midrule
1 file  & $2{,}555$ & $9{,}070$ & $0.992$ & $0.691$ & $\mathbf{+0.301}$ \\
2 files & $593$     & $3{,}688$ & $0.963$ & $0.734$ & $+0.229$ \\
3 files & $111$     & $1{,}010$ & $0.973$ & $0.697$ & $+0.276$ \\
4+ files & $49$     & $809$     & $0.694$ & $0.621$ & $+0.073$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The binary measure is positive in \emph{every} stratum. The share-based gold measure is
$+0.070$ pooled but only $+0.020$ after standardising over patch width --- $72\%$ of the gap is
composition --- and flips sign inside strata 2--4. We therefore quote the binary measure and refuse
to quote the share.}
\end{table}

\subsection{Three mechanisms tested; all three falsified}
We did not accept ``the metric is broken'' without trying to say \emph{how}.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Confident wrongness} --- a run fixating on a \emph{wrong} file scores high.
  \textbf{Falsified:} runs that never reached a gold file score \emph{lower} ($0.661$) than those
  that did ($0.679$), the $95\%$ CI excluding zero on the wrong side.
\item \textbf{Patch breadth} --- failed runs write broader patches ($2.20$ files vs $1.35$),
  enlarging their own target set. \textbf{Falsified as the explanation:} standardising over patch
  width makes the gap \emph{larger} ($-0.088 \rightarrow -0.125$), and the gap is already largest in
  the narrowest stratum, where breadth cannot operate.
\item \textbf{Fixation} --- the metric rewards concentrating edits on one file. \textbf{Partly
  supported, not the mechanism:} failed runs are more fixated ($0.779$ vs $0.727$) and the most
  fixated runs of all are those that never reached the right file ($0.818$ vs $0.759$) ---
  independently corroborating \cite{confidentwrong2026} --- but fixation correlates no more with the
  broken metric ($0.251$) than with the gold metric ($0.294$).
\end{enumerate}
The honest conclusion is a \textbf{construct-validity} one, not a mechanism one. We demonstrate the
inversion, prove the metrics agree where they must, and show the correction restores agreement with
the literature. We cannot explain it. The mechanism remains \textbf{open}.

\subsection{Two independent replications on eight unseen shards}
\label{sec:repl}

The frozen study used Nebius shards $0$--$3$. Shards $\mathbf{4}$--$\mathbf{7}$ and
$\mathbf{8}$--$\mathbf{11}$ were separately downloaded and parsed into their own tables, so no
replication touches the frozen artifacts, with gold patches resolved independently ($74.6\%$ and
$77.9\%$ coverage). Together the three tables cover \textbf{12 of 12 shards and $80{,}035$ runs}:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccccc}
\toprule
set & shards & runs & \code{self} solved\,$\to$\,failed & \code{gold} solved & failed & $p$ & wrong-fix \\
\midrule
frozen     & $0$--$3$   & $26{,}679$ & $0.594 \to 0.670$ & $0.477$ & $0.407$ & $7.0\times10^{-3}$ & $67.0\%$ \\
held-out A & $4$--$7$   & $26{,}680$ & $0.593 \to 0.677$ & $0.492$ & $0.421$ & $3.6\times10^{-3}$ & $69.0\%$ \\
held-out B & $8$--$11$  & $26{,}676$ & $0.614 \to 0.686$ & $0.512$ & $0.415$ & $\mathbf{2.8\times10^{-5}}$ & $66.4\%$ \\
\bottomrule

\caption{Three disjoint sets of $\sim$26{,}000 runs each. The broken metric inverts in every one;
the gold target reverses it back in every one, with the paired test \emph{strengthening} on unseen

\end{table}

\paragraph{Scope, stated plainly.} This is \textbf{not} a merged $80$k-run table. It is full shard
coverage delivered as one frozen table plus two held-out replications --- methodologically stronger
than a merge, since each replication is genuinely unseen, but it means the pipeline was never
re-run on one $80$k table. The earlier single-table attempt failed (two builds raced on one output
file) and that failure is recorded rather than hidden. \textbf{No claim rests on a merged table.}

\section{Where failures actually are}
\label{sec:where}

With a target independent of the agent, failure splits mechanically:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{llr}
\toprule
failure mode & definition & share \\
\midrule
\textbf{WRONG-FIX} & failed, but \emph{did} edit a gold file & $\mathbf{67.1\%}$ ($10{,}947$ runs) \\
LOST & failed and never edited a gold file & $30.9\%$ ($5{,}380$ runs) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent Stable across model scale ($66.8\%$ Llama-70B, $70.7\%$ 8B, $70.2\%$ 405B) and across gold
patch widths ($64.8$--$74.0\%$). Because only the binary measure is stratum-robust:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{$98.2\%$ of successful runs and $69.1\%$ of failed runs reach the correct file. Localisation
tooling can address at most the $30.9\%$ of failures that never get there; the remaining $69.1\%$
happen with the agent already in the right place.}
\end{quote}

This is a \emph{bounded-lever} claim and it is weaker than ``localisation does not matter'' --- which
is what our retracted result implied. The corrected version is also the one that agrees with the
published literature instead of contradicting it.

\section{The causal test}
\label{sec:causal}

\S\ref{sec:where} is correlational. So we intervened, holding the defect fixed and changing
\emph{only} how easy it is to find.

\paragraph{Design.} Real pure-Python packages, no Docker, the packages' own test suites as verifier.
Three arms, same tasks, same model, same budget: \code{unhinted} (a defect exists; find and fix it),
\code{hinted} (told the exact file \emph{and} function --- localisation solved by fiat), and
\code{verify} (unhinted plus ``after a failed test, re-diagnose before changing more code'').
Predictions were \textbf{fixed in advance} from \S\ref{sec:where} so a failure could not be
reinterpreted afterwards.

\paragraph{Two harness faults had to be fixed first, both caught by reading transcripts, not scores.}
(i) \textbf{$65.2\%$ of turns were unparseable}: our first protocol was ad-hoc text, but the model
emits its own XML tool-call format regardless, so the pilot measured \emph{protocol compliance}, not
debugging. Native function-calling reduced this to $0$--$4\%$. (ii) \textbf{The agent was writing its
own tests into \code{tests/}}, which pytest collects --- an agent-authored passing test could have
scored a run as \emph{successful without the defect being fixed}. The verifier is now restored to
the package's original tests before final grading, as a real grader does.

\paragraph{Results.} The larger run used a $48$-task suite ($8$ real packages, $2$ arms, $96$
episodes, \$0.91). Auditing the raw episode records --- not the summary --- showed $13$ episodes
where the task was \emph{not actually broken at the start}; those are excluded. On the remaining
$83$:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lrcccc}
\toprule
arm & $n$ & success & $95\%$ CI & reached gold & \textbf{success $\mid$ gold} \\
\midrule
\code{hinted}   & $40$ & $\mathbf{0.550}$ & $[0.40,0.70]$ & $\mathbf{1.000}$ & $\mathbf{0.550}$ \\
\code{unhinted} & $43$ & $\mathbf{0.372}$ & $[0.23,0.51]$ & $\mathbf{1.000}$ & $\mathbf{0.372}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Live experiment, temperature $0$.}
\end{table}

\noindent\textbf{Every run in both arms reached the correct file --- and $62.8\%$ of the unhinted
runs still failed.} In this suite localisation was never the barrier at all, and the binding
constraint is plainly the fix. This is the causal counterpart of the observational $69.1\%$ /
$98.2\%$ gap, under a manipulation that removes search cost entirely.

\paragraph{P1 held.} Handing over the exact file and function raised success by $+17.8$ points, and
the \emph{failure-rate} drop, $0.178$, is below the $0.309$ ceiling \S\ref{sec:where} predicted in
advance.

\paragraph{The effect is not statistically significant, and we say so.} Fisher's exact test on $40$
vs $43$ episodes gives $\mathbf{p = 0.126}$. P1 was pre-registered as an \emph{inequality about
magnitude} precisely so it could be checked without significance; a reader must not read it as a
demonstrated improvement.

\paragraph{P3 held.} $100\%$ of runs reached the file and a majority still failed. An earlier,
smaller run ($16$ tasks, $3$ arms, $50$ episodes) gave the same shape and carried the \code{verify}
arm: hinted $0.438$, verify $0.353$, unhinted $0.235$, with success-given-gold $0.583$ / $0.400$ /
$0.286$.

\paragraph{P2 failed and we report it as a failure.} Telling the agent to re-diagnose after a failed
test did \emph{not} help; hinting was the strongest arm. The most obvious practical intervention this
study could recommend does not work, and this is the second time a ``spend the budget on
verification'' intuition failed under test in this project.

\paragraph{A behavioural finding of its own.} In $\mathbf{71}$ of the $83$ valid episodes the agent
created or modified files under \code{tests/} --- $1{,}048$ files removed by the verifier restore in
total. Agents write their own tests constantly while debugging. Because the verifier is restored
before grading, an agent that ``fixes'' a test to make it pass is still scored as failing; but the
frequency is itself a measurement, and it is exactly why our first harness would have produced a
\textbf{wrong success measure}.

\section{A router that works, on a target where the field has no signal}
\label{sec:router}

If $69.1\%$ of failures happen in the right file, a runtime system facing a struggling agent should
decide: spend on \emph{search} or on \emph{verification}? We predict which situation it is in, using
\textbf{only the first $10$--$60\%$ of a run}.

\paragraph{Integrity.} The prefix is steps $0 \dots L-1$; run length appears in no feature. A
self-test that overwrites every post-cutoff step confirms all $112$ feature columns are unchanged
--- it caught a genuine leak, now fixed. Folds are task-disjoint; every method is scored on identical
rows and folds.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
target & $10\%$ & $20\%$ & $40\%$ & $60\%$ \\
\midrule
failure prediction                          & $\mathbf{0.693}$ & $\mathbf{0.721}$ & $\mathbf{0.721}$ & $\mathbf{0.731}$ \\
\quad position baseline                     & $0.667$ & $0.664$ & $0.666$ & $0.666$ \\
\quad output-shape (AgentStop-style)        & $0.554$ & $0.565$ & $0.594$ & $0.607$ \\
\textbf{lost vs wrong-fix}                  & $\mathbf{0.676}$ & $\mathbf{0.701}$ & $\mathbf{0.724}$ & $\mathbf{0.751}$ \\
\quad \textbf{every} baseline               & $0.407$--$0.493$ & $0.411$--$0.490$ & $0.413$--$0.486$ & $0.413$--$0.539$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Baselines beaten in all $8$ cells, bootstrap CIs excluding zero. On the
lost-versus-wrong-fix target every published-style heuristic --- loop, burst, redundancy,
output-shape --- is \emph{at or below chance}.}
\end{table}

\paragraph{A correction to our own prior claim.} Our earlier finding that ``step index beats every
learned monitor'' is invalid: at a fixed prefix fraction the position baseline is \emph{exactly the
run's own length} (AUC matches run length to $\le 0.006$), a hindsight quantity no online system
could use. Against a length-matched control the learned model holds at $0.63$--$0.76$ and strictly
dominates run length.

\paragraph{Calibration, done honestly.} Our earlier ``zero false alarms at every budget'' claim was
\textbf{circular} --- label and detector were one statistic, so the numbers reported were literally
an oracle's. Here labels come from gold patches and detectors from prefix features, against an
explicit null.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
level & achieved false alarms & detection \\
\midrule
$\alpha = 0.05$ & $\mathbf{0.046}$ (worst split $0.061$) & $0.160$ \\
$\alpha = 0.10$ & $0.078$ & $0.258$ \\
$\alpha = 0.20$ & $0.123$ & $0.386$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent $8$ of $8$ levels controlled on the mean. A raw $0.5$ threshold flags $98.4\%$ of
never-failing runs; the calibrated rule flags $\mathbf{3.39\%}$. A formal sequential e-value test is
valid and \textbf{never fires} at $\alpha \le 0.05$ --- reported as a negative, not hidden.

\paragraph{The deployable result} is the routing decision. Scoring $1$ for a correct route:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
policy & value ($\lambda = 0.25$) \\
\midrule
\textbf{this router} & $\mathbf{0.764}$--$\mathbf{0.800}$ \\
always verify        & $0.732$ \\
random               & $0.656$ \\
always search        & $0.518$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent It wins at every prefix fraction and every cost setting --- $12$ of $12$ cells.

\paragraph{Limits we state rather than bury.} Recall at a $5\%$ alert budget is $\approx 0.058$ for
\emph{every} method including the free baseline (base rate $0.837$), so the usable output is a
\textbf{ranking}, not a tight alarm. Gold matching is basename-only, $4.10\%$ of edit steps carry no
filename (biasing LOST upward), and the prefix cutoff uses hindsight, so the fully-online claim
rests on the length-matched control.

\section{Two instruments that did not deliver}
\label{sec:negative}

\subsection{The taxonomy does not transfer across scaffolds}
\label{sec:xscaffold}

We ported the mechanical labelling to four other corpora, first proving the port faithful by
re-running it on SWE-agent and reproducing the frozen rates \textbf{exactly} (absolute difference
$0.0$ on all three rates, $236{,}137$ edits, $25{,}681$ runs).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
scaffold & editor footer & kept & revised & \textbf{dead\_end} \\
\midrule
SWE-agent (frozen reference) & $\mathbf{0.958}$ & $0.157$ & $0.652$ & $0.191$ \\
OpenHands (SWE-Gym) & \textbf{absent} & $0.734$ & $0.229$ & $\mathbf{0.037}$ \\
PI agent & absent & $0.821$ & $0.060$ & $0.118$ \\
mini-swe-agent-plus & absent & $0.489$ & $0.276$ & $\mathbf{0.236}$ \\
multi-framework mix & absent & $0.706$ & $0.115$ & $0.179$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The taxonomy does \emph{not} replicate. Dead-end rate spans a factor of $6$ across
scaffolds.}
\end{table}

\noindent Two conclusions, both negative and both load-bearing. First, \textbf{the instrument
depends on a footer that exists in exactly one framework}: \code{[File: \dots\ (N lines total)]}
appears on $95.8\%$ of SWE-agent edit steps and $\mathbf{0\%}$ of the others, so an edit's
\emph{effect} is unmeasurable elsewhere and every mechanical number here is bounded to frameworks
that print comparable state. Second, \textbf{the dead-end rate is scaffold-specific, not a property
of coding agents} --- it ranges from $3.7\%$ to $23.6\%$, a factor of $6$. The $19.1\%$ headline is
SWE-agent's number and must never be quoted as ``coding agents waste 19\%''.

A scope correction emerged from the same work: $16.7\%$ of SWE-agent edit steps sit in runs that
produced \textbf{no patch at all}. Restricted to runs with a non-empty patch --- the strictly
comparable scope --- the reference becomes \textbf{kept $0.189$ / revised $0.624$ / dead\_end
$0.187$} ($196{,}651$ edits, $22{,}194$ runs). Both scopes are correct for their purpose; only the
second is comparable across scaffolds, and every figure in this paper is labelled with which it uses.

\subsection{Per-step test outcomes are too sparse to test the central claim}

Per-step test results would be the instrument that could falsify our central claim. We built the
parser and validated it hard: of $730$ observations that provably contain test output,
$\mathbf{730/730}$ recovered a verdict, \textbf{zero misses, zero false positives}. It still cannot
answer the question, because \textbf{the data is too sparse} --- only $0.55\%$ of steps and $4.4\%$
of runs contain a test outcome. It does find a real signal (runs that saw a test outcome passed the
evaluator $3.0\%$ of the time against $0.72\%$, a $\sim 4\times$ effect) but at $4.4\%$ coverage it
cannot falsify anything. Reported as a negative with a signal.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.3em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Small $n$ in the causal experiment} ($16$--$17$ per arm). The robust results are the
  pre-registered inequality (P1) and the conditional success rates (P3), not pairwise $p$-values.
\item \textbf{The task suite is single-defect mutations in eight real packages}, not SWE-bench
  instances: a controlled instrument, not a benchmark.
\item \textbf{The mechanical instrument requires a comparable editor footer}; cross-framework
  generality is \textbf{not} claimed.
\item \textbf{The inversion's mechanism is unexplained} --- three candidates tested, all failed.
\item \textbf{Gold matching is basename-based}, and $4.10\%$ of edit steps carry no filename,
  biasing the LOST share upward.
\item \textbf{The prefix cutoff uses hindsight} ($L=\mathrm{round}(f\cdot n_{\text{steps}})$), so the
  fully-online claim rests on the length-matched control.
\item \textbf{Per-step test outcomes are too sparse ($4.4\%$ of runs)} to test the central claim
  from within this data.
\item \textbf{Temperature-$0$ inference is not deterministic}: published measurement finds
  $\sim 9\%$ of per-instance outcomes flip across repeats, so the within-instance split is reported
  with runs-per-instance (mean $21.9$).
\end{itemize}

\section{Use of AI tools}
\label{sec:ai}

A large language model (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) was used as a \textbf{research assistant} throughout:
writing analysis code, running statistical tests, fetching and verifying literature, and drafting
this manuscript. It also served as the \emph{subject} of the causal experiment in
\S\ref{sec:causal}.

It was \textbf{not} used to generate data. All measurements come from public trajectory corpora and
from real test suites executed locally. \textbf{Eight of its own claims were retracted when
falsification tests succeeded}, and every retraction, bug and reversal is recorded in the project's
assistance log with the evidence that caused it; the ledger in \S\ref{sec:retract} is a direct
product of that record. The authors reviewed all code, verified all citations against primary
sources, and take responsibility for the final claims.
